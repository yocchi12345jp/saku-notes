How to Choose Your First Newsletter Tool: A Solo Creator's Checklist — Kit vs. Systeme.io Compared From Official Pricing

I post on social media every day. But I still don't have an email list of my own.

Social platforms help me reach readers. Email could give them another way to follow my work.

I wanted a tool for collecting subscribers, publishing newsletters, and keeping the option to move elsewhere.

So I compared Kit and Systeme.io using a practical checklist.

Prices checked on November 8, 2026. All prices and plan details come from official pricing and help pages.

This is the checklist I used to choose my first newsletter tool. I am not a paying user of either service yet. This article is not a ranking.

On November 8, 2026, I signed in to both free plans. On Kit, I added myself as a subscriber, created a draft landing page, and sent a test broadcast to myself. On Systeme.io, I tried to send a test campaign but could not complete it.

That is the full hands-on scope. I tested nothing beyond these actions: no deliverability rates, open rates, or paid-plan experience.

Here is how I decided.

The automation distinction matters.

Unlimited broadcasts do not necessarily mean automated emails. And seeing automation listed on a pricing page does not prove your intended workflow is supported.

For ordinary newsletter publishing, the decision is more straightforward.

Step 1: Decide what the email is for

Before comparing prices, I separated email into three jobs.

Publish

Someone discovers my writing on social media, joins through a form, and receives future newsletters.

That requires a landing page, a subscriber list, and broadcasts. A complicated sales funnel is not necessary.

Automate

Someone subscribes and automatically receives a welcome message or a scheduled email sequence.

That is different from manually sending broadcasts. Free plans may support one without supporting the other.

Sell

Someone moves from an email to a digital product, course, or paid offer.

Payment features, funnels, and transaction fees become relevant here.

Systeme.io combines funnels, courses, and selling tools. Kit also permits digital products and subscriptions on Free.

My immediate job is publishing, not rebuilding an entire sales system. That determines which features deserve priority.

Step 2: Check the free runway

A free plan needs enough capacity for your actual work.

Here is what the official pricing pages showed.

Kit Free: more room for newsletter subscribers

Kit calls its free plan Newsletter.

It includes:

  • $0/month, up to 10,000 subscribers.
  • Unlimited broadcasts.
  • Unlimited landing pages and forms.
  • Tagging and segmentation.
  • Digital product and subscription selling.
  • One user.

That gives a newsletter-focused creator room to collect subscribers without immediately paying.

But Kit Free has no visual automations and no automated email sequences.

The official comparison table confirms both exclusions.

Some secondary articles claim Kit Free includes one basic automation. That conflicts with the official table.

For manually published newsletters, the restriction would not block my intended workflow. For automatic welcome sequences, it would.

Systeme.io Free: more types of tools

Systeme.io describes its free plan as free for life, with no credit card required.

It includes:

  • $0/month, up to 2,000 contacts.
  • Three funnels.
  • One course.
  • One blog.
  • One custom domain.
  • Unlimited email sends.
  • One email campaign.
  • One automation rule.
  • Up to 500 students.

This is a broader package with a smaller contact allowance.

Unlimited email sends does not mean unlimited campaigns or automation rules.

The free plan limits those to one each.

A creator experimenting with funnels and courses might value that package. Someone primarily building a newsletter may not need those tools yet.

Kit offers the longer free subscriber runway. Systeme.io offers more business-building functions within tighter contact and campaign limits.

Neither free plan should be assumed to provide the exact automated welcome workflow you want without further verification.

Step 3: Check what changes when you grow

Free-plan allowances tell only part of the story. Paid features may eventually become necessary.

Kit Creator pricing

Kit's official pricing page showed:

Subscriber count Monthly billing Annual billing
1,000 subscribers $39/month $33/month, $390/year
10,000 subscribers $139/month $116/month, $1,390/year

At 1,000 subscribers, monthly billing costs $39/month. Annual billing is displayed as $33/month, with a stated total of $390/year.

At 10,000 subscribers, monthly billing costs $139/month. Annual billing is displayed as $116/month, totaling $1,390/year.

Check the annual total rather than relying only on the displayed monthly equivalent.

Kit Free already supports up to 10,000 subscribers. Moving to Creator is therefore not necessarily about exceeding the free subscriber allowance.

Features excluded from Free, including automated sequences, also affect that decision.

Systeme.io Startup pricing

Systeme.io lists Startup at $17/month with monthly billing (up to 5,000 contacts), or $170/year total with annual billing, advertised as two months free.

The verified pricing information does not establish separate Systeme.io prices at 1,000 and 10,000 contacts, so I cannot make a precise comparison at those sizes.

Some secondary articles quote $27/month for Startup. The official monthly-billing figure I checked was $17/month.

Watch the billing-period trap

A comparison can be misleading when one tool's annual-billing monthly equivalent is placed next to another tool's actual monthly-billing price.

Those represent different commitments.

Compare prices using the same billing period. Check the annual total and the subscriber or contact count attached to each quote.

A lower entry price does not establish the cost at your future list size.

Step 4: Check the fee you don't see in the plan price

Subscription fees are not the only cost to consider if you intend to sell.

Kit's transaction fee

Kit's official pricing page states a transaction fee of 3.5% plus 30 cents per transaction on all plans, inclusive of card processing.

Kit Free permits digital product and subscription selling, but transactions still carry that fee.

This is separate from the subscription price.

It does not determine my immediate publishing decision. I will reconsider it if I start selling through Kit.

Systeme.io's transaction fee

Systeme.io advertises a 0% platform transaction fee on all plans.

That does not establish that every payment has zero cost.

The official pricing page I checked did not state whether separate Stripe or PayPal processing charges apply.

I would verify the payment-processing arrangement before selling.

For now, the documented difference is clear. Kit states a transaction fee including card processing. Systeme.io states no platform transaction fee, while separate processing charges remain unverified.

There is no reason to invent a sales projection or break-even point before either becomes relevant.

Step 5: What I actually found on the free plans

On November 8, 2026, I signed in to both services and checked a limited publishing workflow.

Kit: I completed a test broadcast

My Kit account already existed, so I moved it to the free plan rather than registering again.

I entered no card details and set the display name to Saku Mochizuki.

Then I added myself as a subscriber.

The subscriber name field accepted only a first name, so the record showed Saku.

Immediately afterward, the subscriber list briefly displayed zero subscribers under its default filter. Changing the filter to All showed my one subscriber.

Next, I created a landing page draft from a template in five clicks.

It saved automatically. I did not publish it.

Finally, I sent a broadcast titled:

Test — my first broadcast on Kit

The recipient was my one subscriber: myself.

For this test, no additional verification, domain setup, or account review was required.

The email arrived in my Gmail inbox within the same minute.

That confirmed I could complete this particular free-plan sending workflow. It did not establish general deliverability performance.

I also found details that would need attention before public publishing.

The From name appeared as my email address rather than Saku Mochizuki.

The footer automatically included an unsubscribe link, a US postal address, and "Built with Kit".

After sending, a notice said I could edit or stop the send within the next 60 minutes. I did not test those actions.

The basic path from subscriber to landing page draft to test broadcast was completed.

Systeme.io: I could not complete the sending test

I signed in to Systeme.io. The dashboard confirmed Free at $0/month.

My own contact already existed, created when the account was made. Its status was Unsubscribed.

Adding the same email address again was refused because it was already in use.

I edited the existing contact's name to Saku Mochizuki.

Then I tried preparing an email.

In both Campaigns and Newsletters, the Sender email address field had no selectable options.

I added my Gmail address under Settings > Emails.

It appeared as Verified, but with a warning icon.

When I returned, the sender field still had no selectable address. No explanation appeared on screen.

In my test, I could not create a draft or send a campaign. Zero emails were sent.

This does not mean Systeme.io can never send from Gmail addresses. I did not establish why sender selection failed in my account.

I also opened the funnel creation dialog without creating a funnel.

It displayed a name field, an automatically filled funnel domain, and four purposes:

  • Build an audience
  • Sell
  • Custom
  • Run an evergreen webinar

The currency defaulted to Euro.

My first impression was that the next step was obvious.

Other defaults were less convenient in Japan. The phone country code showed +33, the currency was Euro, and pages sometimes loaded slowly.

What the test established

I completed a free-plan test broadcast on Kit.

I could not complete the equivalent sending test on Systeme.io under the conditions I encountered.

That matters for my immediate publishing goal.

It does not establish that Kit has better deliverability, that Systeme.io is generally unreliable, or that either paid plan performs differently.

The finding is narrower: one tested workflow worked for me that day; the other did not.

Step 6: Fit it to a simple workflow

My publishing routine uses an AI-assisted drafting process.

Generation and fact-checking are separate. Publishing decisions and responsibility for factual accuracy remain with me.

I do not need a newsletter platform to replace that process.

I need a simple connection between a checked draft and a reader's inbox.

That means usable forms or landing pages, a short route from draft to send, and a way to export subscribers.

Keep subscriber export on the checklist

Both services document CSV export.

Kit allows subscribers to be exported to CSV. Its official help page says the download link arrives by email and expires after 5 days.

Systeme.io allows contacts to be exported to CSV. Its official help page says the emailed file remains downloadable for only 3 days.

Download exported files before those links expire.

I want the option to leave my first platform without abandoning my subscriber list.

CSV export provides a documented starting point, although it does not guarantee an effortless migration.

I have not tested either export process. These details come from the official help documentation.

My decision and what would change it

I am choosing Kit as my first newsletter tool.

This is a decision about my workflow, not a universal ranking.

My immediate task is to give the writing I already publish daily on social media a home in email.

Funnels and courses already live elsewhere in my workflow, including note and similar platforms.

I do not need to recreate those systems inside a newsletter service.

Kit Free supports up to 10,000 subscribers, giving me the longer free publishing runway.

I also completed the basic sending test.

Those reasons are enough for my current decision.

I will reassess Kit's transaction fee if I begin selling through the platform.

Two changes could make me reconsider the tool.

First, if I needed funnels, a course, and payments together, Systeme.io would deserve another look.

Second, if free automated welcome emails became essential, I would revisit both services and verify the exact workflow before choosing.

The purpose is not to predict every future requirement.

It is to choose a tool that handles the current job while preserving room to change direction.

Checklist: 7 questions to answer before you sign up

  1. How many subscribers or contacts can I keep for free? Kit Free supports up to 10,000 subscribers. Systeme.io Free supports up to 2,000 contacts. Compare the limits against your actual publishing plans.

  2. Can I automate welcome emails on the free plan? Check sequences, automation rules, and campaign limits separately. Do not assume an automation label guarantees your intended workflow.

  3. What will I pay at 1,000 and 10,000 subscribers or contacts? Obtain official prices for the relevant list sizes. Do not extrapolate contact-specific costs from an entry price.

  4. Am I comparing the same billing periods? Compare monthly billing with monthly billing. If considering annual billing, check the full yearly total.

  5. What transaction fees apply when I sell? Separate subscription charges, platform fees, and payment-processing costs that still need verification.

  6. Can I export my contacts or subscribers to CSV? Check how files are delivered and when links expire. Confirm the migration requirements before relying on export alone.

  7. What is the main job this tool must do now? Publishing, automation, or selling? Choose around that requirement rather than the total number of available features.

You do not need every feature to send your first newsletter.

For me, the immediate task is moving a fact-checked draft into a reader's inbox.

Kit is the tool I chose for that job.
